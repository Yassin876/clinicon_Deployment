Topic: Anemia (الأنيميا / فقر الدم)
Category: Hematology

Definition:
Anemia is a condition where the blood lacks enough healthy red blood cells or hemoglobin to carry adequate oxygen to the body's tissues.

Types:
1. Iron Deficiency Anemia: Most common type. Caused by insufficient iron intake, blood loss, or poor absorption.
2. Vitamin B12 Deficiency Anemia: Due to lack of B12, often from poor diet or pernicious anemia.
3. Folate Deficiency Anemia: Due to insufficient folic acid.
4. Sickle Cell Anemia: Inherited condition where red blood cells are abnormally shaped.
5. Thalassemia: Inherited blood disorder with reduced hemoglobin production.
6. Aplastic Anemia: Bone marrow fails to produce enough blood cells.
7. Hemolytic Anemia: Red blood cells are destroyed faster than they are made.

Symptoms:
- Fatigue and weakness
- Pale or yellowish skin
- Shortness of breath
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Cold hands and feet
- Fast or irregular heartbeat
- Chest pain
- Headache
- Brittle nails
- Craving non-food items (pica)

Diagnosis:
- Complete Blood Count (CBC): Hemoglobin, Hematocrit, MCV, MCH, MCHC
- Normal Hemoglobin: Males 13.5-17.5 g/dL, Females 12.0-16.0 g/dL
- Ferritin level (iron stores)
- Serum iron and TIBC
- Vitamin B12 and folate levels
- Peripheral blood smear
- Reticulocyte count

Treatment:
Iron Deficiency:
- Ferrous Sulfate 325 mg (1-3 times daily)
- Iron-rich foods: red meat, spinach, beans, fortified cereals
- Vitamin C enhances iron absorption
- IV iron for severe cases or malabsorption

B12 Deficiency:
- B12 injections (intramuscular)
- Oral B12 supplements
- B12-rich foods: meat, fish, dairy, eggs

Folate Deficiency:
- Folic acid supplements (1 mg daily)
- Folate-rich foods: leafy greens, citrus fruits, beans

When to See a Doctor:
- Persistent fatigue not improved by rest
- Hemoglobin below 7 g/dL (may need blood transfusion)
- Symptoms of severe anemia: chest pain, rapid heartbeat, fainting
