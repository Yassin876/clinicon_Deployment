Topic: Diabetes (مرض السكري)
Category: Endocrinology

Definition:
Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disease characterized by elevated blood sugar levels due to insufficient insulin production or the body's inability to use insulin effectively.

Types:
1. Type 1 Diabetes: An autoimmune disease where the immune system attacks insulin-producing cells in the pancreas. Usually diagnosed in children and young adults. Requires lifelong insulin therapy.
2. Type 2 Diabetes: The most common type (90% of cases). The body becomes resistant to insulin or doesn't produce enough. Often linked to obesity, sedentary lifestyle, and genetics.
3. Gestational Diabetes: Occurs during pregnancy and usually resolves after delivery, but increases the risk of developing Type 2 diabetes later.

Symptoms:
- Frequent urination (polyuria)
- Excessive thirst (polydipsia)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Extreme fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing wounds
- Numbness or tingling in hands and feet

Diagnosis:
- Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS): Normal < 100 mg/dL, Prediabetes 100-125 mg/dL, Diabetes >= 126 mg/dL
- HbA1c: Normal < 5.7%, Prediabetes 5.7-6.4%, Diabetes >= 6.5%
- Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT): Diabetes >= 200 mg/dL after 2 hours
- Random Blood Sugar: >= 200 mg/dL with symptoms

Treatment:
- Type 1: Insulin injections or insulin pump
- Type 2: Lifestyle changes (diet + exercise), Metformin (first-line), Sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, Insulin if needed
- Target HbA1c: < 7% for most adults

Complications:
- Diabetic retinopathy (eye damage)
- Diabetic nephropathy (kidney damage)
- Diabetic neuropathy (nerve damage)
- Cardiovascular disease
- Diabetic foot ulcers
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) — emergency

Prevention:
- Maintain healthy weight
- Regular physical activity (150 min/week)
- Balanced diet low in refined sugars
- Regular screening for at-risk individuals
